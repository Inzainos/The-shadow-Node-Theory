## Abstract

This paper presents Shadow Node Theory (SNT), a formal model of node satellization
across three scales of systemic resolution — Micro (Atomic Node), Meso
(intra-national Fungal Network), and Macro (superorganism collision). The central
hypothesis holds that when two power nodes orbit in critical proximity, the node
with greater accumulated advantage satellizes the historically dominant node
through a power-law algorithm R(t)=a·t^b invariant to temporal scale and substrate.
This version supersedes SNT v2.3.1: the previously posted 502-case corpus contained
synthetically generated values and is retired; it is replaced by a corpus of 721
cases reconstructed entirely from verifiable primary sources (R²∈[0,1] for every
case; 89% significant). Three robust findings emerge: (1) institutional friction is
the dominant predictor of satellization velocity (Spearman ρ=−0.68, p=2.5×10⁻⁹⁷,
n=714); (2) friction-free biological domains satellize ~10× faster than
friction-laden economic ones (Mann-Whitney p=2.4×10⁻⁷⁴); and (3) abrupt triggers
are faster than gradual ones (U=24,802, p=1.91×10⁻⁵, n=486), stable across three
corpus expansions. SNT also delivers the Triple-Resolution Model, N-body
verification with INEGI data (b=−0.473, R²=0.838), and operationalization of the
Atomic Sovereignty Index (precision=1.0). This revision adds a Coupled Orbital
Collapse layer (ACO-A): collapse as an orthogonal axis (Δ), a falsifiable hazard
h(τ)>0, a taxonomy of collapse modes (friction × trigger × floor/ceiling), and a
Principle of Least Friction, demonstrated with real data in five domains (finance,
history, crypto, biology, astronomy). The golden-ratio hypothesis (H-φ) was tested
and refuted across four rounds and is excluded from the claims. Eight falsifiability
criteria, a four-step diagnostic protocol, and a public replication package
accompany the model (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.19446521).
