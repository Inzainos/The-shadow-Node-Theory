To the Editors of Journal of Complex Networks,

I am submitting the manuscript entitled:

  "Scale-invariant satellization: a power-law model of node dominance
   verified across 502 empirical cases in 11 domains"

for consideration as an Original Research Article.

─── WHY THIS JOURNAL ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Journal of Complex Networks is the primary venue for this work. The paper
formalizes satellization — the progressive extraction of productive resources
from a peripheral node by a dominant hub — as a power-law dynamic R(t) = a·t^b,
and verifies it across 502 cases spanning historical city-pairs, national
economies, biological invasions, parasite-host systems, and astrophysical
structures. This directly addresses the journal's mandate: "mathematical,
physical and computational principles for studying complex networks and their
applications." The paper connects the preferential attachment literature
(Barabási & Albert 1999) to a specific mechanism — satellization — that has
not been formally quantified across empirical domains before.

─── SIGNIFICANCE ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Three findings are statistically robust across three successive corpus
expansions (57 → 114 → 502 cases):

1. Abrupt triggers produce satellization 5.9× faster than gradual ones
   (Mann-Whitney U = 24,802, p = 1.91 × 10⁻⁵, n = 486).

2. Institutional friction is the primary predictor of the exponent b —
   more than substrate, time scale, or domain — verified across 11 domains
   spanning 30 orders of magnitude of temporal scale.

3. Political sovereignty and ecological mutual interdependence are
   mathematically equivalent as satellization brakes (b_countries = −0.098,
   n = 230 sovereign pairs; b_predator-prey ≈ 0 under stable conditions).

The Atomic Sovereignty Index (ASI = δH·α/F) achieves ROC-AUC = 0.715 on
first-session behavioral data (n = 4,774 users, HackerEarth 2026), with
zero false positives at the ASI ≥ 1.0 threshold.
